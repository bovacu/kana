# Hindi data sources: licence research (2026-10-04)

The full research behind `docs/hindi_thai_data.md`, for a paid, closed-source
app teaching Hindi in the Devanagari script. It is checked against primary
sources: licence files, official terms pages, download pages and the live
data. Counts were measured on the live files unless marked "quoted".
Anything not confirmed at the source says "unverified". This is a licence
reading, not legal advice.

The downloaded pages, licence texts and samples are in
`data/raw/research/hi/` (git-ignored); the Tatoeba exports, ML Kit pages,
font licences and sentence-corpus READMEs shared with Thai are in
`data/raw/research/common/` (`tatoeba2/`, `fonts_hi_th/`, `sentences/`,
`mlkit_*.html`).

The rules applied:
- no NonCommercial or NoDerivatives licences;
- ShareAlike only where it covers the data and not the app's code;
- GPL, LGPL or AGPL data kept out of the app.

### Indian copyright in government works

From the Copyright Act, 1957 (official PDF, copyright.gov.in; saved as
`levels_india_copyright_act_1957.pdf`):
- s.2(k): "'Government work' means a work which is made or published by or
  under the direction or control of— (i) the Government or any department of
  the Government…"
- s.17(d): "in the case of a Government work, Government shall, in the absence
  of any agreement to the contrary, be the first owner of the copyright
  therein".
- s.28: copyright "shall subsist until sixty years from the beginning of the
  calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published."
- s.52(1)(q) permits reproducing only "(i) any matter which has been published
  in any Official Gazette except an Act of a Legislature", Acts with
  commentary, reports laid before a legislature, and "(iv) any judgment or
  order".

So **NCERT textbooks, government syllabi, primers and handbooks are
copyrighted** (60 years from publication) and are not covered by s.52(1)(q).
They can be cited as references for facts such as stroke order; their
drawings and text cannot be copied.

### US government works (FSI)

17 U.S.C. §105: "Copyright protection under this title is not available for
any work of the United States Government"; §101 defines it as a work
"prepared by an officer or employee of the United States Government as part of
that person's official duties". Works by contractors are not automatically
covered.

## 1. Stroke order and shapes

**No usable machine-readable stroke data exists for Devanagari** (nothing like
KanjiVG or Make Me a Hanzi), and the recorded-pen datasets are research-only.
We draw the strokes ourselves, as for Hangul. The order to follow has an
authoritative government source, the Central Hindi Directorate's chart
(§1.3), with open Wikimedia Commons animations to cross-check it.

### 1.4 The rules

#### 1.4.1 General rules

1. **The letter's body first, then the vertical bar (danda), then the head
   line (शिरोरेखा) last.** In every letter of the CHD chart the build-up
   stages never include the head line; it appears only on the finished letter,
   with an arrow. Opiaterein, JackPotte and Saurmandal agree. High.
2. **The head line is one stroke, left to right.** Over थ ध भ (and over the
   right part only of अ आ ओ औ) it covers only the danda part: the contrasts
   घ (full head line) / ध (partial) and म (full) / भ (partial) must be drawn.
3. **The danda is drawn top to bottom, after the body part to its left.** For
   क फ ब ष it comes **before** the hook or diagonal on its right (CHD).
4. **Body strokes start at the top**: usually at a short stem hanging from the
   head-line position (इ उ घ ङ छ ट ठ ड ढ द ह र ...) or at the top left (अ ख ग प
   म ल ...), and flow down in one movement where possible.
5. **Dots come after the head line**: the CHD chart draws the head line of ङ,
   ड़ and ढ़ before the dot or nukta. By extension: ं, the dot of ँ, ः and ़.
6. **Marks above the head line are drawn before it**, starting at the head
   line and going up to the free end (the marks of ऐ ओ औ, the curl of ई; CHD
   arrowheads, Saurmandal's start markers).
7. **Loops** follow the CHD arrows and Opiaterein's frames: क's lobe
   counter-clockwise from the top right near the stem; ठ's circle
   counter-clockwise; the "३" of अ/ओ: the upper bowl clockwise from the top
   left, flowing into the lower bowl and ending at the lower left.

#### 1.4.3 Vowel signs, signs, digits, half-forms

- **Vowel signs (matras) and signs** (ा ि ी ु ू ृ े ै ो ौ ॉ ् ं ँ ः ़): **no
  authoritative open source shows their order**; CHD does not cover them (HP
  Labs only assumed "vowel modifiers … always occurred as distinct strokes").
  House rules extended from CHD: ा is a danda drawn downward after the
  consonant; े ै (and the marks of ो ौ) from the head line upward; the curls of
  ि ी start at the head-line junction; ु ू ृ after the body (before or after
  the head line: open; CHD's ऊ draws its tail before it); dots last. CHD:
  where a vowel sign already sits above the head line, chandrabindu is written
  as a plain dot ("जहाँ स्वरमात्राएँ शिरोरेखा के ऊपर लगती हों वहाँ … चंद्रबिंदु के
  स्थान पर केवल बिंदु", as in में). **Whether ि is written before or after its
  consonant is unverified** (only blogs say "consonant first"): ask a teacher.
- **Digits ०–९:** CHD shows the forms (p. 9: the Hindi forms of ५ ८ ९, as in
  Unicode Table 12-8; it also notes that Article 343(1) makes international
  numerals the official ones). **No stroke-order source:** house-defined.
- **Half-forms** (CHD 2024 §3.1):
  - 3.1.1 "खड़ी पाई वाले व्यंजनों के संयुक्त रूप परंपरागत तरीके से खड़ी पाई को
    हटाकर ही बनाए जाएँ।" (letters with a danda lose it);
  - 3.1.2.1 "क और फ / फ़ के हुक के आधे भाग को हटाकर संयुक्ताक्षर बनाए जाएँ"
    (क and फ lose half the hook);
  - 3.1.2.2 "ङ, छ, ट, ठ, ड, ढ और ह के संयुक्ताक्षर हल् चिह्न लगाकर बनाए जाएँ।"
    (these take the virama), except द्य द्व, which keep their ligatures;
  - 3.1.2.3 the three र forms stay: the repha (र्क), the subscript (क्र) and
    the ट्र/ड्र form;
  - 3.1.2.4 "'श्र' का यही प्रचलित रूप मान्य होगा … त् + र के संयुक्त रूप के लिए 'त्र'
    ही मानक";
  - so a half-form is the letter's body strokes without the danda, and the
    head line is drawn once across the whole conjunct at the end.

#### 1.4.5 Letter forms

The CHD tables use the modern Hindi forms: अ as "३" plus bar, झ as an इ-like
body plus bar, ण as U plus bar, and the Hindi ल and श; they match the Hindi
column of Unicode Table 12-8. The Marathi/Nepali forms of झ ल श ५ ८ ९ are
Unicode allographs not used for Hindi; the older forms (the "Bombay" अ, the
old ण and झ) are not in the CHD chart (not researched further). The 2016 rules
(knots in ग न म; छ's shape; त्र and श्र) were not found in the 2024 text, but
the chart is the same in both editions: follow them.

## 2. Character metadata

### Unicode

- **Devanagari block** U+0900–U+097F: 128 assigned characters in Unicode
  18.0.0 (extract in `unicode_devanagari.txt`). Names are of the form
  "DEVANAGARI LETTER KA", "DEVANAGARI VOWEL SIGN I", "DEVANAGARI SIGN
  ANUSVARA".
- The Unicode Standard, chapter 12 (18.0.0; `unicode_core_ch12.html`) defines
  how the script is stored and rendered, and is the reference for the
  composed forms the app must draw:
  - half-forms: "These half-forms do not have an inherent vowel. Their rendered
    forms in Devanagari often resemble the full consonant but are missing the
    vertical stem, which marks a syllabic core";
  - the RA rules: R6 (a dead consonant before RA takes the subscript RA, ठ्र),
    R7 (some combine into ligatures, फ्र), R2 (repha above), R14 (रु रू are
    special shapes);
  - R9: "the nukta sign ... is placed immediately after the consonant ... If
    the consonant represents a dead consonant, then NUKTA should precede
    VIRAMA";
  - ZWJ and ZWNJ: "क् + ZWNJ + ष → क्‌ष" (no conjunct), "क् + ZWJ + ष → क्‍ष"
    (half-form).
- CLDR has a Devanagari-to-Latin transform (InterIndic-Latin): a letter-by-
  letter transliteration with no schwa deletion. Not a learner romanization.

### Letter names

Devanagari letters are named by their sound: consonants with the inherent
vowel (क "ka"), vowels by their sound (आ "ā"); vowel signs as "ā kī mātrā"
(the ā sign), "choṭī i kī mātrā" (short i), "baṛī ī kī mātrā" (long ī); ्
halant (virama), ं anusvār / bindu, ँ chandrabindu, ः visarg, ़ nuktā. These
are conventions, not anyone's text. Primers pair each letter with a picture
word (अ से अनार, क से कबूतर ...), like Thai's ก ไก่; the words vary from primer
to primer, so the app picks its own.

English Wiktionary (via kaikki, §3) has an entry for each letter with its
order in the alphabet ("the first consonant in Hindi"), romanization and IPA:
अ a /ə/, आ ā /aː/, ऋ ŕ /ɾiː/, ऑ ŏ /ɒ/ ("used to transcribe English"), अं ã,
अः aḥ, क ka /kə/, ख kha /kʰə/.

**Schwa deletion is the central problem.** Devanagari writes an inherent "a"
after every consonant without a vowel sign; Hindi drops it at the end of words
and in some middle positions (कमल kamal, not kamala; समझना samajhnā). A
letter-by-letter scheme (ISO 15919, IAST, CLDR) gets this wrong. Wiktionary's
romanizations already apply it, so dictionary words need no rule; sentences
and new words need our own rules plus an exceptions list.

## 7. Word splitting and text issues

Hindi puts spaces between words, so the word splitter is simple: split on
spaces and punctuation (danda । U+0964 is the full stop, ॥ U+0965 the double
danda). Remaining issues:
- **Postpositions** are written apart after nouns (राम ने) but joined to
  pronouns (उसने, मुझको); dictionary lookup needs the form-to-lemma map
  (Wiktionary's 14,696 form entries) or a small lemmatiser for verbs.
- **Nukta:** U+0958–U+095F (क़ ख़ ग़ ज़ ड़ ढ़ फ़ य़) are composition exclusions
  (`CompositionExclusions-18.0.0.txt`: "0958 # DEVANAGARI LETTER QA" ...), so
  NFC keeps them decomposed (क + ़). But ऩ ऱ ऴ (U+0929, U+0931, U+0934) do
  compose under NFC. The wiki data is NFC with decomposed nukta (fr 572, pt
  127, ja 232 decomposed; 0 precomposed). NFC-normalise all data and input.
  Many writers drop the nukta (जरूर for ज़रूर): fold it in search.
- **ZWJ/ZWNJ** select half-forms or block conjuncts (found in the data: 6
  ZWJ in kaikki fr). Keep for display; strip from search keys.
- **Spelling variants:** anusvara vs a nasal cluster (हिंदी / हिन्दी), and
  chandrabindu vs anusvara (हँसना / हंसना). Fold both for lookup.
- **Schwa deletion** (§2) affects romanization and the voice, not the
  splitter.

## Best combination

1. **Strokes:** our own centreline strokes in KanjiVG-format XML, as for
   Hangul (the plan is in `docs/hindi_thai_data.md`): the 11 vowels (+ अं अः
   ऑ), 33 consonants + क्ष त्र ज्ञ श्र + ड़ ढ़ (ळ optional), the nukta letters,
   the vowel signs and signs, the digits, the half-forms and the र forms,
   following the CHD 2024 chart (order and direction) and its §3.1 rules
   (half-forms), cross-checked against the Commons animations; letters drawn
   on the CHD five-line grid. Syllables (consonant + vowel sign, the बारहखड़ी)
   and conjuncts composed in code, with one head line drawn last.
2. **Dictionary:** English Wiktionary (via kaikki) at the core: romanization
   with schwa deletion, IPA, English, the form-to-lemma map. es/pt/fr/ja: the
   native Wiktionary entries, Wikidata's CC0 sense labels (~1,500 senses each),
   Wiktionary translation tables, then our own labelled translations.
3. **Romanized input:** Dakshina (CC BY-SA) plus the filtered Aksharantar
   rows, to accept how learners type.
4. **Sentences:** Tatoeba (through English for es/pt/ja/fr, labelled), MASSIVE
   for short sentences in all five languages.
5. **Frequency:** wordfreq (`small_hi`), with variants folded.
6. **Levels:** by frequency and theme; KHS course names or ACTFL descriptors
   only as inspiration.
7. **Romanization:** our own IAST-like learner scheme with schwa deletion,
   tested against Wiktionary's output; optional plain-ASCII mode.
8. **Lessons:** Wikivoyage in en, es, fr, pt (Japanese our own), FSI's Active
   Introduction, UT Austin's own dialogues and glossaries (CC BY 4.0),
   Wikibooks.
9. **ML Kit:** handwriting `hi`, photo text `DevanagariTextRecognizerOptions`,
   translation `hi`.
10. **Fonts:** Noto Sans Devanagari for the interface; Playpen Sans Deva for a
    school-like look, Kalam for a handwritten one.
11. **Publish** the converted CC BY-SA data (Wiktionary, Dakshina if used), as
    for Kana.

## Gaps

- No open stroke data: ours to draw (high confidence). No authoritative source
  for the order of vowel signs (especially ि) or digits: house rules, checked
  by a teacher.
- es/pt/fr/ja meanings: ~1,500 Wikidata senses and a few hundred to 1,279
  native Wiktionary words each; the rest through English.
- No Japanese Wikivoyage phrasebook.
- No official graded list.
- Tatoeba: 62% of the sentences by one contributor; only 49–278 direct
  translations into es/pt/ja/fr; no audio.
- No reusable recorded audio beyond Wiktionary's 802 words (Commons, per file)
  and UT Austin's glossaries.

## Risks (with confidence)
